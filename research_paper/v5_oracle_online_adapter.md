# Blokee v5 Oracle online-adapter experiment

**Started:** 2026-08-28  
**Status:** measured; prediction gate failed; search integration not authorized

## Hypothesis

The first learned trajectory bracket improved the population policy but failed to transfer player
identity: a different holdout player's recurrent state predicted just as well. The follow-up keeps
the learned legal-action scorer but removes the fitted history-to-readout map. Instead, a latent
mixture learns eight policy experts offline and a parameter-free Bayesian filter infers their
weights separately for each seat during the real game.

This separates two jobs:

- network weights learn what plausible Blokus policies look like across a population;
- Bayes' rule, likelihood tempering, posterior drift, and surprise reset adapt those policies to a
  previously unseen player from observed actions only.

No adapter output is connected to move selection in this phase.

## Fresh expanded league

The new corpus begins at seed 52,600,000 and is never mixed with the once-read holdout from the
first learned-policy experiment. It uses 24 whole profiles:

- 12 training profiles spanning greedy/search behavior, v1/v2/v3 engines, exploration endpoints,
  aggression endpoints, and move-budget shape endpoints;
- 6 validation profiles for online-filter hyperparameters and calibration only;
- 6 once-read holdout profiles containing interpolated exploration/aggression settings, a later
  move-budget shape, and v4.1.

Each game contains profiles from exactly one split. Profiles and games are therefore both disjoint.
The export contract remains exact legal candidates, within-set standardized action features, zero
missing played actions, and finished games only.

## Fixed model and controls

The model has a shared candidate trunk and eight expert readouts. It is trained as a sequence-level
latent mixture: a player's trajectory is generated by one coherent expert, rather than choosing a
new expert independently at every move. Expert-use balance and readout diversity are regularized to
prevent collapse.

At inference, every seat begins at the uniform expert prior. The validation block alone selects
likelihood learning rate, posterior drift, reset threshold, and one global policy temperature.

Holdout controls are:

1. **fixed:** never update the uniform expert prior;
2. **adaptive:** update the current seat's posterior after each real action;
3. **shuffled:** use another profile's online posterior at the same turn index;
4. **stateless:** the calibrated population scorer from the same candidate trunk.

## Preregistered gate

Metrics begin after three prior observations. Games are the bootstrap unit. Root-sampled belief
search is authorized only if all conditions hold on the once-read holdout:

- adaptive NLL beats fixed with a 95% lower bound above **0.03 nats/action**;
- adaptive NLL beats shuffled with a 95% lower bound above **0.06 nats/action**;
- top-five recall exceeds **65%**;
- ten-bin top-one ECE is at most **0.08** and no more than 0.01 worse than fixed;
- all experts receive at least 3% mean posterior responsibility on training, preventing a nominal
  eight-expert model from collapsing to a population scorer;
- a validation-selected surprise reset is non-inferior to no reset on unseen holdout profile
  splices, and its last four predictions are within 0.10 nats/action of clean history.

Passing authorizes only a belief-MCTS implementation experiment. Playing strength still requires a
fresh equal-wall-clock comparison with v4.1-ultra and profile-shuffle attribution. Failure sends the
next cycle to richer board/action features and human trajectory collection.

## KV-cache boundary

This adapter has no transformer prefix, so a KV cache would add no value. Runtime state is eight
posterior probabilities per opponent plus interaction state. If a later transformer clears the
same identity gate, it should use one immutable real-history KV prefix per opponent; branch-local
hypotheses must use copy-on-write deltas or a frozen latent snapshot and never alter that prefix.

## Results

`MEASURED` The expanded export satisfied its contract: 108 finished games, 24 disjoint profiles,
7,738 decisions, 1,555,396 exact legal candidates, and zero missing played actions. The eight-expert
model had 1,632 parameters and selected epoch 114 using validation only. The validation filter was
temperature 1.10, likelihood learning rate 0.25, posterior drift 0.07, and surprise threshold 6.25.

On the once-read holdout, after three observations per seat:

- adaptive NLL: **2.835**;
- fixed-prior NLL: 2.845, an adaptive gain of **+0.010 [0.003, 0.017]** against a required lower
  bound above 0.03;
- shuffled-posterior NLL: 2.842, an identity gain of
  **+0.007 [−0.000, 0.014]** against a required lower bound above 0.06;
- top-five recall: **59.4%** against a required 65%;
- top-one ECE: **0.070** versus fixed 0.079, so calibration passed;
- minimum mean final expert responsibility: **9.2%**, so all eight experts were active and the
  diversity gate passed.

The surprise threshold caused zero resets on both validation and holdout splices. Its nominal gate
passes because keeping the contaminated posterior was already within 0.002 nats/action of clean
history. As in the preceding recurrent experiment, this is vacuous: the inferred player state was
too weak for contamination to hurt.

Three of six gates fail, including both primary likelihood gates. Root-sampled belief search is not
authorized.

## Diagnosis and boundary

The transfer mechanism was not the missing piece. Replacing the fitted recurrent state with exact
online Bayes moved the correct-profile comparison back into the positive direction, but only by
0.007 nats/action. Expert collapse is ruled out by the balanced 9.2–13.6% responsibilities, and
calibration is acceptable. The remaining bottleneck is observability: nine standardized scalar
features describe *what kind of option* was selected but omit most of the board pattern, piece
geometry, future corridor structure, score context, and target-specific spatial relation that can
distinguish two strategically different reasons for the same scalar signature.

The next Oracle data contract must therefore add information before model capacity:

1. candidate-local spatial patches or compact bitboard planes;
2. piece/orientation identity and remaining-piece schedule;
3. target-specific corner-denial, corridor, and territorial effects rather than only totals;
4. exact search-policy evidence such as visit share or value gap when the source is a CPU;
5. consented, player-disjoint human trajectories, because CPU configuration diversity is not a
   substitute for human retaliation, persistence, or local conventions.

No further transformer, recurrent, or response-search experiment is justified on the nine-feature
contract. KV caching remains a valid transformer inference optimization, but it cannot recover
identity information that the input does not contain.
