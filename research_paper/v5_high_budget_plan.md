# Improving Pro and Ultra at equal wall time

Research proposal, November 17, 2026. The native v5 ledger and experiments referenced here are on the local `v6-native-engine-e4` branch, inspected at `c04a918`. The checked-out `main` contains the older v5 Oracle work. The recommendations below are proposed experiments. Round 10 already has its own preregistration.

## What the runs establish

- **Version lineage.** v5.0 put a fitted placement prior, prior-initialized full expansion, RAVE, priorLog selection, rank/quality reward and last-good-reply together over sampled playouts. v5.1 tuned that package. v5.2 calibrated its iteration scale by tier on the M4 Pro. v5.3 changes how many prior-ranked replies an interior node may consider as its visits grow. Nano and Lite use `floor(2 + 0.5√N)`. Base and Turbo use `floor(4 + √N)`. Each earned its name on fresh seeds.
- **Benchmark.** One Blokee seat plays three Pentobi 31.0 seats, book on, one thread per Pentobi seat, four rotations per seed. Advantage is the lone seat's score minus the mean of the other three. The interpolated break-even levels for v5.3 Nano, Lite, Base and Turbo are 3.01, 3.53, 5.16 and 6.15, against runtime targets L1, L3, L5 and L6. Base and Turbo had heavier in-match load than their predecessors. Use clean matched costs to compare implementations.
- **Pro.** v5.2-Pro scored 6.67 [6.53, 6.83], with −2.02 [−3.14, −0.84] points against L7. Round 9's wider cap, W1D-ET (`floor(16 + 4√N)` with its iteration scale retimed), improved on its paired baseline by +1.16 [+0.41, +1.91] over 300 seeds. Its absolute result against L7 was −0.37 [−1.10, +0.37]. Round 10 is confirming it on 800 fresh seeds. Use the candidate as the next control only if it is accepted.
- **Ultra.** v5.2-Ultra scored −4.14 [−8.55, +0.68] against L9 on 11 complete seeds. It used 15.3 s/move versus Pentobi's 18.9 s/move in that match. L8 is missing, so Ultra's break-even level and the size of its equal-time deficit are open questions.
- **Scaling.** In the round 7 probe, roughly 5,280 → 47,609 iterations bought 4.14 → 5.60 mean plies. In round 8's 50-position Pro probe, the narrow W1C reached 7.33 plies and W1D reached 5.78. Yet W1D was the more promising high-budget match arm. Deeper search helps only if the necessary replies remain accessible. At Pro, only 1.3% of visits survived to the next own turn in the reuse diagnostic.

The source of record is `docs/V5_TRACKER.md`, `docs/BENCHMARK.md` and rounds 6–10 of `research_paper/v5_1_candidates_results.md` at the research commit above. The current checkout also has the [benchmark output](../tmp/bench/v5.3-cand-nano/bench.md), [Pro screen](../tmp/v56/pro/screen.md) and [scaling probe](../tmp/v54/B/scaling.md).

## Experiment order

### 1. Test whether a remaining piece will still fit after the opponents reply

**First Pro strength experiment.** Sample positions from own turns 9–16 in completed development games, balanced over seats and including wins and losses. For each of the top eight plausible root moves, apply it and play the next three eligible opponents in their real turn order, stopping at the mover's next decision. Sample several plausible reply sequences with the same seed schedule across candidate moves. Count remaining pieces with zero, one or several distinct viable attachment regions. Two orientations relying on the same vulnerable corner should count as one opportunity. Directly playing a scarce piece removes its risk. Keep the score sensitive to the size of the exposed piece and to the completion bonus. Losing the last current fit is evidence of risk because later moves can reopen access.

Start with a development probe comparing (a) the current search, (b) counting fits immediately, and (c) counting fits after the reply sequence. Force the candidate move and play independent continuations for all four seats to estimate how often the piece actually gets stranded and what it costs in final margin. Advance the reply feature to a match only if it adds information beyond immediate counts on held-out games and changes enough decisions to matter. In the match arm blend one clipped feature into the root fitted prior, retain the full legal root, and cap the probe time, initially at 5% of the move budget. Use the native fitted-root path in `engine-rs/src/search.rs`, reusing placement and move generation. Track terminal margin, all-21 completion, stranding by shape, and end-to-end time.

This deliberately revisits a failed area. The old root-only static feasibility feature confirmed at +0.13 [−0.96, +1.39]. The native N19 mobility refit won its screen, lost confirmation, and cost about 15× when applied throughout the tree. The new question is whether *opponent replies destroy the last independent fit*. The comparison with immediate counts decides whether the extra work earns its place. Multi-player playout search is a research precedent for sampling responses. This feature and its proposed use are Blokee hypotheses. [Earlier feasibility experiment](v32_eight_hour_shift.md) · [Nijssen & Winands, playout search](https://dke.maastrichtuniversity.nl/m.winands/documents/acg2011paper.pdf)

### 2. Let the width of an interior node depend on its position

**Second Pro strength experiment.** Compare one adaptive window against the accepted control. Keep the root fully searchable. At expanded interior nodes compute the effective number of moves in the fitted prior, `1 / Σp²`. Bin the result using development positions. Initially use two widths around the control's width at the same visit count. Admit more replies when prior mass is diffuse, fewer when concentrated, and keep widening monotonically with visits. If round 10 fails, include W1D as a diagnostic comparator while keeping v5.2-Pro as the promotion control.

Match the adaptive arm's mean eligible width to a fixed-width-schedule control by visit band, then time-match both. On independent positions, record the rank of the actual next L7 move, whether it fell outside the window, and how often the tree reaches the mover's next turn. Judge the experiment by paired game margin. A sharper prior can be confidently wrong. Check misses separately at board contact and in cramped positions. This extends the v5.3 result with one testable mechanism. Chaslot et al. studied progressive unpruning in Go. The position-dependent rule here is an adaptation. [Progressive Strategies for Monte-Carlo Tree Search](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/progressive-strategies-for-monte-carlo-tree-search/)

### 3. Make Ultra's existing search cheaper, then measure its strength

**Ultra engineering experiment.** The profiler found a largest tree of 8.35 GB, peak process memory of 10.8 GB, and about 37% of worker time in selection. W1 limits the selection scan but still constructs the whole ordered interior move list and its bookkeeping. Build the lazy allocation noted in round 8. Keep sorted move IDs and priors, grow child IDs and visit counts as the window opens, and preserve RAVE credit for moves played in simulations before their edge opens, for example with a sparse pending accumulator. Preserve update order, transpositions and tie-breaking. Compare with the same cap and eager storage at fixed iterations. Identical ranked-root digests, lower peak memory and faster clean searches justify spending the savings on extra iterations. Memory savings alone can make more benchmark games feasible.

Calibrate v5.2-Ultra and a broad W1D transfer separately on a warm, quiet machine. Use the source-of-record moves-5+ metric alongside total CPU and wall time per game. Screen at L8 against the same opponent and paired seeds for each arm, then test the winner against L9. Budget the L9 run using the pilot variance and commit a fresh fixed sample size in advance. The project measured about four hours per 24 L9 seeds *per arm* with three simultaneous games. A short pilot estimates cost and variance. A claim about L9 requires enough fresh seeds to resolve the chosen margin. [Round 8 profile](../tmp/v55/speed/speed.md) · [Ultra baseline](../tmp/v53/S2/tiers.md)

### 4. Give Pro and Ultra a different source of strategic judgment

**Longer-horizon alternative.** First reuse the existing compact E6 root policy as a single evaluation per searched move, blending it with the current fitted v5 root prior. Confirm that the native fitted-root path consumes it and test a zero-blend identity. On the older v4.2 tree the root-only model confirmed at +0.83 [+0.56, +1.09], while depth-1 and child-value additions failed to establish a gain over the root-only control. Re-test that checkpoint before collecting new labels. If it improves the modern tree, train a student on difficult v5 positions with independent high-budget teacher searches and terminal four-seat outcomes. Hold out entire games and opening families. Charge inference, feature extraction and model startup as applicable on the deployment hardware.

AlphaBlokus supplies direct four-player evidence for a policy/value search with a four-seat result vector. Its author reports 63 wins or ties for AlphaBlokus and 33 for maximum-level Pentobi in 85 games at 2,000 AlphaBlokus simulations. Ties overlap and the hardware and time budgets were uncontrolled. It is a promising independent teacher or validation opponent. A full neural tree is a separate architecture experiment after the cheaper root test. [AlphaBlokus methodology and benchmark](https://github.com/ShivamSarodia/AlphaBlokus#gameplay)

**If the loss instead localizes to the opening**, compare paired continuations from the same positions after all seats' third move, then test an offline opening book for frequent positions under turn-order-preserving symmetries. The native Pro configuration bypasses search on its first three moves. The earlier O0 screen simply switched on search from move one, at extra cost, with an inconclusive result. A learned lookup tests a different use of compute. Report book hit rate on held-out openings, whole-game time and the official moves-5+ time, which excludes the opening expense. [Pentobi engine documentation](https://github.com/enz/pentobi/blob/main/pentobi_gtp/Pentobi-GTP.md) · [Active Opening Book Application](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/active-opening-book-application-for-monte-carlo-tree-search-in-19/)

## Decision rules

1. **Close round 10 under its existing rules.** All 800 complete paired seeds count for strength. The sleep amendment uses chunks 1–7 for the timing comparison. At inspection the driver was running from the checkout now on `main`, where its final report scripts are absent. If the checkout stays there, score the merged logs using the complete script tree from `c04a918`, with the existing `tmp` data visible in its working directory. Run `v51b_report.py --roll-confirm tmp/v57/pro 800` and `v57_pro_report.py`. Verify a real decision line before interpreting the driver's completion message. Follow the preregistered L6 fill-in and conditional L8 amendment if accepted.
2. **Use a separate development corpus.** Exclude round 10, its reserved anchor seeds and future confirmation blocks from selection and training. For new experiments freeze one mechanism, its baseline, seed lists, cost calibration and outcome before starting the screen. For each new option verify that turning it off reproduces the existing search.
3. **Screen at Pro, confirm once.** Proposed default for a new Pro mechanism is a 300-seed L7 screen, followed by 800 fresh paired seeds for a winner. Alternate arm order and rotate all four seats. Require a positive lower bound on the seed-bootstrap 95% interval for the candidate-minus-baseline margin, an upper bound of at least zero on the first-place change, and matched measured time. Predeclare a clean timing tolerance (e.g. ±3%) and recalibrate before confirmation if needed. Report direct play against the incumbent as a transfer check. Keep first-place ties and split wins visible.
4. **Measure scaling explicitly.** On the same held-out positions and against a fixed L7 opponent, compare incumbent and candidate at Turbo time, Pro time, and a higher budget. Report the change in candidate advantage as time increases. A constant gain can improve Pro, while a growing gain is evidence of better scaling. For parity with Pentobi, report the *absolute* L7 or L9 margin with its interval and actual times. An interval spanning zero alone does not establish parity. Bracket the break-even level with adjacent anchors when available. Promote each tier independently.

I would defer generic RAVE or exploration sweeps, cross-turn tree reuse, and further static time curves. Those already have negative or inconclusive high-budget evidence. Simulation balancing is a later rollout experiment if new live-game evidence points there. The project's earlier calibration and sibling-ranking proxies selected the wrong known rollout policies, so an offline metric alone would be a poor reason to fund a new rollout family. [Rollout channel audit](v46_channel_audit.md) · [Silver & Tesauro, Monte-Carlo Simulation Balancing](https://icml.cc/2009/papers/500.pdf)
