# D1 — game anatomy of the X1 level-6 games (`v4.2-ultra` vs Pentobi 31.0 level 6)

Descriptive diagnostic, run 2026-11-12. There is no strength claim and no preregistered gate.

**Data.** I replayed both level-6 blocks with exactly the parameters in `engine-rs/scripts/x1-run.sh`: 100 seeds from 79,000,000, budget 300, Pentobi book on, one thread, 6 parallel games. x1-l6a is `--mode one-blokee` and x1-l6b is `--mode one-pentobi`. The replay used the new `--moves-out` flag and wrote to `tmp/d1/`. `tmp/x1/` was not touched.

**Replay check: PASSED.** All 800 replayed games equal the originals by (seed, seat): scores, winner and challenger move count. A second check went through the engine. Every game in the moves files was rebuilt with `GameState`/`Position`, every recorded pass was confirmed to have no legal move, and every final score was recomputed and found equal. `aggregateMatches` on the replays gives the same −10.68 [−11.96, −9.41] and +5.56 [4.59, 6.59]. No game hard-failed.

**Method.** `engine-rs/examples/d1_anatomy.rs turns` rebuilds every position with the engine's own code and writes one row per turn (passes included) and one row per seat per game. The features use `Position::generate_moves`, `open_corners` and `territory::compute_territory`.

- **Live corner:** an open corner that at least one legal move of that seat covers.
- **Killed:** a live corner that stops being live because of the move.
- **Local response:** Pentobi's own definition (`PlayoutFeatures::set_local`). Take the attach points of the last 3 placements on the board, excluding the mover's own. Keep only points not forbidden for the colour that made the placement, and add their orthogonal neighbours.

`d1_anatomy judge` replays a stratified sample of l6a decision positions into a fresh `pentobi-gtp --level 7` and asks it for a move with `reg_genmove`. Pentobi runs with the book on, one thread and seed 1. There are about 1,500 decisions per engine, roughly 70 per own-move index; Blokee has fewer at indices 18–20. `d1_anatomy shortlist` re-searches the same positions with `v4.2-ultra` at budget 300. The tables come from `engine-rs/scripts/d1_report.py tmp/d1`.

Throughout:

- **k** is a seat's own-move index (0–20). Round r is the seat's r-th turn; while a seat can still move, k = r. A seat that cannot move once can never move again.
- **Differences** are Blokee − Pentobi within each game, averaged over the seed's four seat rotations.
- **Intervals** are 95% bootstrap intervals by seed.

## Reading

### Where and how the gap opens (l6a; l6b has the same shape at about half the size)

1. **Rounds 0–3: Blokee is ahead on mobility, behind on reach.**
   - Blokee's first three moves are its fixed heuristic: W5, F5 and X5 in 100% of seats, all unsearched. Level 7 never picks the same move there and picks the same piece 2% of the time. Pentobi-L6 matches level 7 in 38% of those positions.
   - Pentobi mostly opens with the long pieces Z5, V5, N5, L5 and W5. After its second move it is 3.0 from the centre; Blokee is 6.0.
   - Blokee nevertheless has more open corners (+2.9 at round 3) and more legal moves (+68 at round 3).
2. **Rounds 4–9: territory and mobility are lost while piece sizes are still equal.**
   - Territory, Blokee − Pentobi:
     - round 4: −3.0 [−4.2, −1.7];
     - round 9: −7.0;
     - rounds 12–14: about −8.
   - Legal moves turn negative at round 5 (−25 [−38, −12]) and reach −45 at round 9.
   - Blokee still has more open corners until round 8, and piece size is equal through round 8. The squares gap at round 9 is about 0.
   - So the damage is positional and invisible in the score until round 9. More corners with fewer legal moves and less territory means Blokee's corners are worse ones: more cramped, or more exposed.
3. **Rounds 10–14: the gap becomes squares, mostly through smaller pieces.**
   - Size gap per move: −0.40 at k=10, −0.58 at k=11, −0.68 at k=12.
   - Squares gap over the phase: −3.37 [−3.86, −2.91]. About 2.3 of that is smaller pieces, 1.1 is turns lost.
   - Live corners now fall behind: −1.1 at round 11, −4.2 at round 15.
4. **Rounds 15–21: the gap becomes getting stuck.**
   - Share of seats that can still move, Blokee vs Pentobi:
     - round 14: 0.83 vs 0.98;
     - round 16: 0.49 vs 0.91.
   - Squares gap over the phase: −5.49 [−6.09, −4.89], almost all from turns lost.
   - First round with no move: −2.23 [−2.49, −1.98]. Stuck before round 15: 16.8% of Blokee seats, 1.6% of Pentobi seats.
   - Blokee is left with +1.13 four-square and +0.59 five-square pieces more than Pentobi.
   - All pieces placed: 1.0% vs 10.5%, which is −1.73 in bonus.
   - In total: score −10.68 = squares −8.95 + bonus −1.73.
5. **Blokee's corners are the ones that get killed.**
   - In rounds 0–14 a Blokee seat loses +0.68 and +0.81 more live corners to opponents than a Pentobi seat (two phases, rounds 0–9 and 10–14), although it loses fewer legal placements.
   - A Pentobi move kills +0.031 [0.020, 0.042] more live corners on the Blokee seat than on each Pentobi seat.
   - In l6b a Blokee move kills −0.034 [−0.045, −0.023] fewer on the Pentobi seat than on each Blokee seat.
   - So in both blocks Blokee's corners are easier to kill and Pentobi's harder. The data cannot tell whether Pentobi targets Blokee on purpose or Blokee's corners are simply more exposed.
6. **Among Pentobis vs among Blokees.**
   - A Blokee seat does 6.16 [5.19, 7.11] points worse among three Pentobis than among Blokees (l6a vs l6b). It places 5.95 fewer squares, gets stuck 1.41 rounds earlier and has 18 fewer legal moves at round 10.
   - Pentobi hardly changes with its company: −1.04 [−2.08, 0.00], with legal moves at round 10 unchanged.
   - Pentobi opponents hold Blokee's mobility down much harder than Blokee opponents do. Pentobi's own mobility is robust to its opponents.

### Per suspected mechanism (docs/V5_TRACKER.md)

- **P1, local-response rollouts: no evidence either way from the moves; one obvious proxy is contradicted.**
  - In the games, Blokee covers a Pentobi "local" point less often than Pentobi does: 0.260 vs 0.326 over moves 3–15, −0.065 [−0.076, −0.054]. The gap is significant at every k from 4 to 17 except k = 5, and the base rate of local moves among the legal moves is identical.
  - In the same positions, however, level 7 answers locally no more often than Blokee: 0.250 vs 0.249 over moves 3–15. Where they disagree, level 7's moves are not more local (+0.00 [−0.04, +0.03]).
  - So the local-response gap belongs to the positions each engine reaches, not to Blokee declining local answers. "Blokee does not answer local threats" is not supported.
  - This says nothing about whether local-aware rollouts give Pentobi better value estimates, which is what P1 is about. Testing that needs the port itself.
- **P3, rank reward: cannot tell.** Nothing in move-level data separates a rank objective from a margin objective. Pentobi completes the set much more often (10.5% vs 1.0%), but that follows from mobility, and Pentobi's reward does not target the bonus.
- **P4/P5, priors and full expansion: supported. This is the clearest mechanism signal in D1.**
  - At own moves 3–8, level 7's move is in Blokee's 40-move root shortlist in only 45% [37, 52] (k 3–5) and 42% [35, 49] (k 6–8) of Blokee's positions. Blokee cannot choose it in the other half; there are about 300–560 legal moves.
  - Coverage rises to 67% at k 9–11 and 95%+ from k 12. That matches where the losses start: positionally in rounds 4–9, in squares from round 10.
  - When Blokee's move differs from level 7's (moves 3–15, n = 719), it differs in exactly the directions of Blokee's hand ordering terms (`piece_size`, `centre_pull`, `new_corners`, `deny_corners`):
    - bigger piece: +0.15 [0.04, 0.24];
    - closer to the centre: mean cell distance −1.26 [−1.56, −0.95];
    - more own corners: +0.41 [0.29, 0.53];
    - more opponent corners covered: +0.21 [0.13, 0.28].
  - Pentobi-L6's disagreements with level 7 show no centre or blocking bias (centre +0.08 [−0.21, +0.39], covered +0.03 [−0.05, +0.11]).
  - This fits a prior/funnel that over-rewards size, centrality, corner count and blocking, and that cuts the moves level 7 prefers before the search sees them. The tier-1 funnel to 96 uses only size and centre.
  - The judge data cannot separate the prior from the rollout policy or the leaf evaluation, which reward similar features. Full expansion (P4) is the direct test.
  - **Search noise.** Re-searching Blokee's own positions with a different RNG reproduces its played move only 39% (k 3–5) and 50% (k 6–8) of the time. Many middle-game decisions sit on near-ties.
- **P9, opening rules: supported as a large behavioural difference; its cost cannot be isolated.**
  - Blokee's W5/F5/X5 opening is never level 7's choice. It leaves Blokee 3 squares further from the centre after two moves, with −4.6 territory at round 2.
  - By round 3, reach and territory are level again and Blokee has more corners and legal moves. The lasting territory and mobility deficit starts at rounds 4–5, the first searched moves.
  - The opening is a plausible contributor, but the data cannot split its share from the early-search shortlist effect, which operates on the same moves.
  - Pentobi's book is barely used: under 1 ms on about a third of first moves and nowhere after, by the timing flag. Pentobi's opening therefore comes from its search, presumably steered by its centre-distance prior rule; that rule is not observed directly here.
  - The direct test: start Blokee from Pentobi's opening, or search from move 0.
- **Piece order: clear difference; consistent with, not a cause of, the gap.**
  - Pentobi plays the elongated pentominoes first, all at mean index 2.9–3.9: Z5, V5, N5, L5. It plays the compact F5, X5, T5 and U5 later, at 6.3–8.6.
  - Blokee does the reverse: W, F and X at 0–2, then Z, V and L at 6.2–7.2.
  - Blokee plays I5 in 60% of seats vs 88% for Pentobi, and plays the small pieces about 2 moves earlier: I1 13.3 vs 15.6, I2 13.8 vs 15.8.
  - Early small pieces and unplayed 4/5-square pieces are what being squeezed looks like. Holding the long pieces may contribute, because long pieces are what reach through gaps late; the data cannot say how much.

### What I am unsure about

- Level 7 is a stronger player, not ground truth. Its "best move" is one search per position, run from a fresh process without the reused subtree it would have in a game.
- The judge sample is stratified by own-move index, so Blokee has few positions at k ≥ 18.
- By-index rows are conditional on the seat still being able to move, which is survivor selection late in the game. The by-round tables avoid this and are the ones to use for "when".
- The local-point definition follows Pentobi's playout code with passes skipped, as in Pentobi's move list. The prior's version of the local feature (`PriorKnowledge.cpp`) was not reproduced.
- The book flag (Pentobi under 1 ms) is a timing heuristic.
- Territory is Blokee's own Voronoi estimate, not ground truth.
- The shortlist check re-searches with a different RNG from the game. The candidate list is set by the ordering, so it should be the same as in the game; visits are not.
